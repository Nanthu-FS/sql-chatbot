# Dither Farm: owner decisions

Answers the owner gave while scoping the game. `GAME_DESIGN.md` must follow these.

## Platform and presentation
- Native Android (Kotlin, custom Canvas renderer). No Unity.
- Portrait, isometric view. Tap the farm directly (Hay Day style); there is no avatar.
- Pixel art: 32px, Endesga-32 palette, checker dithering, black outline, hard drop shadow. Art round 3 (sharp and clean, inspired by a lush reference but without its blur): a wider palette with hue shifting (shadows cooler, highlights warmer), no dithering (clean steps), coloured outlines, crisp see-through cast shadows to the lower right with sun spots under trees, leaf-cluster foliage, and posterised ground patches with grass clumps. World sprites and items get this from Tools/ArtGen/polish.py; art round 4 gives UI icons, panels, buttons and villager portraits the same clean look (9-slice sizes and corners unchanged). Rocks (picked from three styles): ore-coloured rocks about a farmhand wide and knee high, three shape variants each; ore and stone item icons match. The Mine Mouth is a timber tunnel cut into a grass-topped block that joins the quarry cliffs. Art round 5 (owner pick: a hand-pixeled redo of the main farm, Dither Farm's own painted-toy look): flat bold paint, chunky rounded silhouettes and a lit edge on every top, with three signature marks repeated across the farm: the barn's red-and-cream trim, the wheat-sheaf emblem from the app icon and hand-painted names. Drawn pixel by pixel on the iso grid (Tools/ArtGen/pixel/_iso.py, farm_hand.py), in batches the owner sees first; the pickup truck and its load, the workbench, the well and the market stall came first, then the barn (owner pick of three: DITHER FARM painted on its slate roof, a wheat barn-quilt on the wall, the wheat on the cupola), a cream silo with red hoops and dome, and the orders board; then a yellow combine with a red header and turning reel, slatted crates with a red stamp, cream-railed fences on red posts (neighbouring segments share one chunky post), red field signs in a cream frame and red lamp posts; then the small farm machines (compost bin, bakery oven, preserves shelf, beehive, fruit press, seed maker), each with a cream plate carrying a painted icon rather than a name (owner pick); and the rest: an oil press with a millstone on edge, a striped market cart, a harvest shed (red roof) and a planter shed (green roof), and a cream windmill with a red cap and plain cream sails. Then the yard: the bunkhouse (cream, red roof and shutters, its lit windows drawn on the same canvas), a square hay bale with red twine, a trough on red legs, a plank bridge with red posts and cream rails, and red signposts with painted tree and pick icons. Every farm building, machine, vehicle and prop is now hand-pixeled (the scarecrow was already a hand-drawn map and stays; the greenhouse is drawn in code; the ray-cast models stay in buildings.py, village.py and machines_farm.py for comparison only). All sized to the scale bands. Reference packs the owner shared were looked at for readability only; nothing is copied from them.
- Font: Silkscreen (OFL).
- SFX only, no music. Haptics with a settings toggle.
- App name: **Dither Farm**. The APK is built by GitHub Actions.
- App icon: a red barn with a golden wheat sheaf on sky and meadow, flat pixel art at every density plus an adaptive icon. Store listing: six real screenshots and a feature graphic with the title on the game's wood panel (`Docs/store/`).

## Time and world
- 1 in-game day = 20 real minutes. Progress continues while the app is closed (offline catch-up).
- Day/night lighting.
- Seasons are 14 days long (about 4.7 h real time). Crops wither outside their season; a greenhouse lets any crop grow in any season.
- Weather: rain auto-waters crops. Storms are risky: they can damage unprotected crops, and lightning rods / scarecrows protect them.

## Farm and economy
- Start with 6 plots and buy more land with coins.
- 12 crops: wheat, carrot, potato, tomato, corn, strawberry, pumpkin, sunflower, cabbage, eggplant, melon, grapes.
- Market prices swing daily (about ±30%), shown with arrows.
- Detailed leveling. The player starts with some resources.
- Storage is limited; barn/silo capacity can be upgraded.
- Goals: order board, achievements, collection book.
- Short guided tutorial tips.
- Local notifications when crops or machines are ready (toggle).

## Forest, mine, tools, machines
- Farm, Forest and Mine are separate screens, switched with buttons.
- Mine: a regrowing quarry near the entrance plus descending floors for rare ores.
- Energy bar: chopping and mining cost energy. Energy refills over time (including offline), by eating food, and on level up.
- Tool tiers: axe, pickaxe, watering can, hoe (plus others), going wood → stone → iron → gold.
- Machines: sprinkler, auto-harvester, seed planter, auto-seller, processors (mill, sawmill, furnace), and more.
- Coal fuel for some machines (furnace, drills); farm machines run free.

## Buildings, villagers, saves
- Machines and buildings go into fixed building slots (predefined spots), not free placement.
- Order board orders come from named villagers with pixel portraits; completing their orders earns friendship points.
- Saves are local with an automatic backup copy, plus export/import of the save file. Saves are crash-safe: written to a temp file, forced to disk and renamed into place; the backup only ever holds a whole save; a damaged save falls back to the backup; a save from a newer game version, or a damaged one, is kept as a copy before it is replaced.
- Minimum Android version: 10 (API 29).

## Camera, quality, events
- Every screen pans with drag and zooms with pinch, in pixel-perfect integer zoom steps.
- Performance (no visual change): screen text and small panel frames are drawn once into cached images, off-screen props are culled, and fixed scenery is worked out once; `FrameTimeTest` reports ms per frame for the farm, forest and a mine floor.
- Crops come in normal, silver and gold quality; fertilizer and care raise quality and sell price.
- Events: a travelling merchant (rare seeds and tools), one festival per season with a mini-goal, and a daily login gift.

## Balance (from simulation)
- The XP curve is shorter than the design table from level 4 on (`levels.json` is authoritative). A bot that checks in hourly, 8 times a day, reaches L5 on day 1, about L15 after a week and about L22 after three weeks (`PacingTest`, playtest round 4).
- `ProgressionTest` checks that every item a building, recipe, tool, zone or gate needs can be obtained by the level it unlocks.

## Playtest round 1 (owner feedback)
- **World:** the farm grows to about a 40x40 tile world with a dense forest border (no floating-island edge), a river with a bridge, signposted roads to the Forest and Mine, a village edge with cottages and the order board, and an empty fenced pasture kept for animals.
- **Fields:** land is bought as fenced field blocks (not single plots). Each block gets a name, a crop, a worker and a destination: Truck (sell) or Silo (keep).
- **Workers:** named farmhands with their own look, shown as animated pixel people. A worker assigned to a field harvests ripe crops, replants the field's crop (Silo seeds first, then buys), waters and clears dead plots. Workers level 1 to 25 with coins, getting faster each level. Each eats crops from the Silo daily (1 per day at L1-9, 2 at L10-19, 3 at L20-25, cheapest first) and rests if there is nothing to eat.
- **Harvester and truck:** workers stack the harvest in crates at the field edge. A harvester cart collects crates and loads the truck (Truck fields) or the Silo (Silo fields). The truck waits by the barn; tap it to send it off the map to sell its load, and it returns with the coins.
- **Skills:** 1 skill point per level across five branches (Green Thumb, Woodsman, Miner, Trader, Tinkerer), plus craftable one-shot tonics. Respec at the Workbench for coins (price grows with level).
  - Each branch has four skills that unlock in order (one rank of the previous skill); most take 3 ranks, Extra Slot 2. Effects per rank: Quick Sprout +4% growth, Bountiful 6% extra crop, Star Grower better quality, Deep Roots +10% dry growth; Sharp Edge +5% axe crits, Timber 10% extra wood, Seed Saver 8% seed back, Light Swing 10% free axe swings; Prospector 6% extra ore, Ladder Sense ladder 2 rocks sooner, Geologist 4% geode, Steady Hands 10% free pick swings; Haggler +3% sell price, Bulk Buyer -5% seeds, Charmer +5% order coins, Merchant's Pal -5% merchant prices; Overclock +4% machine speed, Fuel Saver -10% coal, Extra Slot +1 queue slot, Big Trays +2 producer batches. Reset costs 100 coins per level.
  - Tonics (Workbench TONICS tab, used from the Barn): Energy Brew +40 energy (L4), Growth Tonic every growing crop +2 hours (L6), Rain Totem waters every crop (L8), Lucky Charm 10 minutes of better quality and extra crops (L11).
- **Scale:** crops are redrawn smaller (about two thirds) so buildings tower over the fields.
- **World scale (round 1b):** the cottage (about 44px) is the yardstick. Farmhands are about 25px, vehicles about a farmhand tall, waist-high things (workbench, compost bin, beehive, anvil, kettle) at most 26px, people-sized machines at most 34px, sheds and big machines stay under the cottage; only the barn, silo, windmill and drill rig are taller. Buildings, machines and vehicles are re-rendered at their size by `Tools/ArtGen/scaled.py` (part of `generate.mjs`), and `ScaleTest` fails the build if a sprite leaves its band. The `farm_scale` screenshot shows the farmstead for a visual check.
- **Farmhand look:** style D3 "tall" (11x21 body, hand-placed pixels: collar, buttons, pocket, knee patches, boot soles), picked from five options (`Docs/farmhand_styles.png`).
- **Audio:** sound effects vary in pitch and have variants; ambient loops (birds, wind, crickets, forest, mine drips); weather ambience (rain, thunder, snowy wind); lo-fi, very calm background music (replaces the earlier "SFX only"), with toggles.

## Playtest round 2 (owner feedback)
- **Fixes:** long toasts wrap to two lines; a locked or for-sale field shows its level or price on its sign board; street lamps get a small soft glow (no painted ground circle) and house windows light at night; tall art never covers panel titles.
- **Energy:** planting, watering and harvesting are free; chopping and mining still cost energy.
- **Unlocks (hybrid + guided):** one guided quest at a time (Grandpa's notebook) with a pointer and a reward. Tools, the quarry and mine, forest zones and machine blueprints unlock by doing (the stone axe within about 15 minutes, after chopping); machines also need their level. Levels still gate crops, machines and buildings, fields and farmhands, villagers and orders. Pace: level 5 on day 1, about level 15 after a week, level 20 within three weeks (round 4; was level 10 after a week).
- **Orders:** only ask for items the player can get right now with their current tools, buildings and zones, in amounts they can make.
- **Tools:** stone tools at the Workbench; iron and gold tools at the village Blacksmith (opens with village tier 1, gold work from tier 2).
- **Big goal: restore the valley.** Four whole-village tiers over about 3-4 weeks: wooden huts, stone, brick, concrete. Each tier rebuilds every house and road (dirt, gravel, cobbles, paved) and the village grows (4, 6, 8, 10 houses). Tiers are paid with bundles of goods plus coins, donated a bit at a time. Rewards: new villagers and orders, farm perks, and shops (tier 1 Seed shop and Blacksmith, tier 2 Carpenter and gold work, tier 3 Cafe). Art variations for the tiers are shown before they are built.
- **Small goals:** a goal chip on the HUD shows a goal the player pins (a village bundle, an achievement, a field or building to save for, chores or the contract); three daily chores each real day with a streak; one truck contract each real week. One chip: the pin wins, else the notebook quest, else the next village bundle. Chores are quick and mixed (one farm, one craft, one orders/chop/rocks), from level 3. The streak forgives one missed day a week, and its bonus grows to day 7. The contract asks for three crops you can grow now, shipped by truck, and pays coins, XP and gold bars or lucky charms.
- **Maps:** the Forest becomes a big map like the farm with a tree wall; the Mine surface and floors are filled (cliffs, cave walls) so no void shows. Forest 44x44: the zones are clearings joined by a trail, with room left for later areas; its edge is trees plus rock cliffs. Mine surface: a quarry with stepped rock cliffs, gravel and scrub, the Mine Mouth at the cliff foot. Mine floors: cave walls with ore glints, fading to black; each band of ten floors has its own colours.

- **Story (round 3):** Goldcrest Bank's smug loan officer, Mr. Sterling, refuses the farmer a loan under the government's Rural Growth Scheme because the village "gives nothing back"; the farmer vows to make the valley the best farming village. Portrait dialogue: the opening in the bank's office, then the officer visits the village after each rebuild (rating C, then B) and at the concrete town approves the loan (50,000 coins) and the valley is named Best Farming Village. The player picks one of four farmer looks and types a name at the start. Older saves skip the scenes behind them; Settings replays the opening.

## Agent playtest (round 3)
- A Haiku agent played 30 turns through the play harness (`Tools/jvm-check/play.sh`). Fixed from its report: taps, holds and slides pick the frontmost tree, crop, rock or building under the finger (not the ground tile behind tall art); chop and mining progress shows as a bar over the tree or rock; tips fit the moment (harvest when something is ripe, else plant) and close by themselves after 10 seconds; the water quest counts all watering so far; a Barn or Silo upgrade says what it still needs; names in panels fit their rows instead of being cut at a fixed length; the quarry gate says the stones come from forest rocks.

## Playtest round 4 (owner feedback)
- Wood was too scarce and trees and quarry rocks took too long to come back: trees regrow about 2.5x faster (pine 12 real minutes, birch 15, oak 30, maple 45, ancient oak 120), a stump rots after one day (was three) and an empty tree spot reseeds at the next dawn (was after two days). Trees drop more wood (pine and birch 3-5, oak 3-4, maple 2-3, ancient oak 10), a stump gives 2 wood (the Logging Hut too), and 3 branches fall a day (5 in a storm, up to 8 lying about). Quarry rocks come back in about 8, 7 and 6 real minutes by quarry level (were 20, 17, 14). After the agent playtest: a stump on a tree's own spot sprouts its sapling 5 real minutes after the fell (no wait for dawn), so a pine is back in about 17 minutes; trees take fewer swings (hp pine 3, birch 4, oak 8, maple 14, ancient oak 40), and a Workbench plank costs 2 wood (was 3).
- Playtest fixes: the water marker over dry crops is a teardrop, not a blue diamond that read as a gem; the new-chores and truck-contract toasts say "Tap the trophy" (the Goals panel opens from the trophy chip; there is no Goals button). The locked Mine button no longer opens the Mine (a Kotlin `?.let { ...; null } ?: nav` slip let it through; `TouchPlayTest` taps it now). The quarry shows its rocks as soon as its notebook step opens it (no hidden level-3 gate that left tapped spots saying "still growing back"), and forest rocks restock 2 stones a dawn, up to 3.
- The Barn and Silo filled too fast: Barn capacity doubled (200 at level 1, 1000 at level 10); Silo 1.5x (110 to 600), since the Silo is what paces a busy farm. The casual bot (`PacingTest`) now reaches L6 on day 1, about L15 after a week and L22 after three weeks (was L10 and L16).

## Round 5 features (owner picks)
- **Farmhands:** one visit tends a whole plot (harvest, clear, replant, water), every 6 real seconds at level 1 and about 10% faster each level (was 12 s for a single action). A level-1 farmhand keeps a 16-plot field in about 1.6 minutes (was about 10). The panel shows PLOTS/MIN. From the agent playtest: dry plots come before empty ones (a dry crop grows slower), a field crate holds 60 (one wheat wave is 48), a full truck sets off for market by itself so Truck fields never stall, and taps on the bunkhouse's walls and roof open the Workers panel.
- **Energy:** the bar is 50% bigger at every level (150 at level 1, 370 at level 30) and refills from empty in 45 real minutes (was 90), online or away.
- **Pet:** at level 4 two strays (a tan farm dog and an orange tabby cat, both in the farm's red collar, hand-pixeled in `pixel/pets.py`) wait by the well, and the adoption panel opens once by itself; the player keeps one and names it (BISCUIT or MISO is filled in). The pet trots after the view on the farm, forest and mine and sits when it catches up. Each dawn it finds one gift (1-3 of: seeds the player can grow, stone, wood, fibre or coins; 8% a quartz, amethyst or old coin), shown by the ripe-crop sparkle over its head, which waits (it does not stack) until tapped; a tap with nothing waiting gets a wag and hearts. Adoption brings a welcome gift.
- **Decorations:** a DECORATE button in the menu (MENU, at the top) opens a shop of ten hand-pixeled pieces (red and sunny flower beds, bench, gnome, birdbath, garden lamp, topiary, lily pond, rose arch, and the Golden Sheaf statue with the farm's wheat emblem), 150 to 4000 coins, some gated by level. One goes on a free grass or yard tile (not a field, road, river, pasture, building or sign) with two taps, like fixtures; tapping a placed one offers to pick it up for half its price. Each adds beauty; every 5 beauty adds 1% to order coins, up to +10%.

## Round 6 features (owner picks, one at a time)
- Backlog in order: better tutorial, giant crops, more decorations, pet extras (fetch, pet levels, accessories), orchard (a fenced plot of 8 fruit trees), farm animals (chickens, cows, sheep in the pasture), fishing in the farm river (timing bar), deep mine adventure, villager friendships, Story Act 2 (a rival agri-corp).
- **Tutorial:** the watering step says how ("tap crops with a blue drop"); a new notebook step after the first order teaches the trophy chip (it blinks gold) and Goals; when the next step is on the farm, the nav bar points at the farm from the forest and mine; tapping the notebook chip takes the player to the target (screen and camera); swapping an order takes two taps; the Orders board explains itself on first open. Save version 5 keeps old saves at the same notebook step.
- **Tutorial, round 2 (playtest):** the field arrow points at the plot the step needs (an empty plot to plant, a dry crop to water, a ripe crop to harvest); entering the forest during the chop or stones step centres the view on a tree the axe can fell or a loose rock, with the arrow on it; a crop's blue drop or sparkle can be tapped like the crop; the Market and Orders steps point at their nav buttons (the quest chip opens them); the harvest step says "when they sparkle".
- **Giant crops:** at dawn a ripe 3x3 square of one pumpkin, melon or cabbage crop has a 15% chance to merge into a giant (hand-pixeled, drawn once over the patch with a sparkle). One tap harvests it: twice what the nine plots would give, all gold, and nine plots' worth of gold XP. Farmhands and the Harvest Shed leave giants for the player.
- **More decorations:** ten more pieces (picket fence, stone and brick paths that lie flat under everything, hay stack, rooster weathervane, stone fountain, and four seasonal ones sold only in their season: sunflowers, blossom tree, pumpkin pile, snowman), so the shop has two pages. Beauty milestones pay once (25: 500 coins, 50: 1500, 100: 5000), and at 100 beauty the farm is "Best Farm in the Valley" for +5% more order coins (15% in all). Decorations and pets now get the same polish pass as the rest of the world.
- **Pet extras:** tapping the pet pats it (love +1 once a day); tapping again throws a stick and it runs to fetch. On the way back a meter swings above it: tap in the green zone for a great catch. The first fetch each day pays 20 + 10 per pet level. Love raises the pet through levels 1-5 (5/15/30/50 love); higher levels find more and rarer gifts. Holding the pet opens its page: level, love bar, gift size, and four accessories (bandana 300, bow 500, straw hat 800, flower crown 1200), each unlocked by pet level and worn on the farm.
- **Pet and decorations, round 2 (playtest):** the fetch meter sweeps at an even, slower speed with a wider green zone, and the pet trots back so there is time to catch; the pet draws over the world so a building never hides it; an arrow marks the strays until one is adopted; the first key typed replaces the suggested pet name; locked accessories show their pet level. Placing a decoration marks every free tile green (a refused tap flashes red) and the shop reopens after each placement; long names are shorter and big pieces shrink to fit their shop card.
- **Orchard:** a fenced plot by the village (between Fields C and E) for sale at level 10 (1500 coins + 10 planks; its sign shows the level, then the price). Eight spots each take a fruit tree bought as a sapling: cherry (L10, 300), apple (L12, 400), peach (L14, 600), orange (L16, 800). Trees grow for 1-2 days, then fruit at dawn every 2 days (cherry, apple) or 3 days (peach, orange) in three seasons and rest in the fourth; every season has fruit. Fruit waits on the tree (tap to pick, +1 XP each) and goes to orders, the Market, the Fruit Press (apple and orange juice) and the Preserves Jar (cherry and peach jam). A tree can be dug up (no refund). Apples are worth 12 now (was 8).
- **Farm animals:** from level 7 the pasture (east of the river, now fenced off the Mine road) takes hens (L7, 250, up to 4: an egg a day), sheep (L11, 800, up to 2: wool every other day, shorn until it grows back) and cows (L13, 1200, up to 2: milk a day). Each dawn they eat from the trough (hen 1, sheep 2, cow 3 feed; wheat gives 1 feed, corn 2; the trough holds 60). A fed animal makes its product, which waits on it under a sparkle; tapping collects it and pets it (one heart a day, up to 5). A hungry dawn costs a heart. Hearts set the quality: silver at 3, gold at 5. The Ranch hand (L15: 4000 coins, 20 planks, 5 iron bars) tops up the trough from the Silo and collects everything into storage each morning. Egg 14, milk 30, wool 70; all go into orders. The farm view now reaches four tiles of forest past the map edge, so the pasture and the field corners can be centred.
- **Fishing:** at level 6 the Workbench makes a fishing rod (6 wood, 4 fibre, 30 coins). Tapping the river casts for 3 energy; the float bobs, then dips: tap within 1.2 s to hook, then tap while the sweeping marker is in the green to land the fish (a miss, a slow tap or three sweeps and it gets away). Eight fish: minnow (any time), perch (spring-fall), trout (spring, fall), bass (summer), salmon (fall), pike (winter), catfish (any season, four times as likely at night and in rain) and the rare golden koi (400 coins). Harder fish have a smaller green zone and a faster marker, and give more XP. Fish sell at the Market and go into orders (not the koi).
- **Deep mine, part 1 (owner picks: tap-to-fight critters, a break-the-armor Golem, crystal tools, treasure rooms, relics, knocked out on defeat):** the mine goes down to floor 80 in four new bands, each with its own colours: crystal caverns (41-50), amethyst depths (51-60), emerald grotto (61-70) and the Golem's obsidian hall (71-80). Crystalite veins (gold pick) appear from floor 41; the Furnace smelts 4 crystalite into a Crystal bar (level 22). The Blacksmith makes a fourth tool tier, Crystal (5 crystal bars, 3 gold bars, a diamond, 4000 coins, level 24): the pickaxe and axe hit for 10 (gold 6); the Crystal hoe adds 5% gold crops. New gates: 50 (level 22), 60 (24), 70 (26, Crystal pick), 80 (28, Crystal pick).
- **Deep mine, part 2 (critters):** floors 41-79 have 1-5 critters (more deeper): bats (2 HP, bite 2, quick and erratic), crystal slimes (3 HP, bite 3) and armoured beetles (5 HP, bite 4, from 51). Once near the ground the player has opened they close in and bite every few seconds. Each tap costs 1 energy and deals 1 (2 with a gold pick, 3 with crystal; crits double). Loot: bats drop coins and sometimes a geode, slimes crystalite, beetles gold ore or a gem. At zero energy the player is knocked out: carried up to the elevator stop above, with a quarter of their energy, and half of every item gained since this trip down began is lost. Critters only act while the mine is on screen with no panel open, never offline.
- **Deep mine, part 3 (treasure and relics):** 18% of floors 41-79 hold a treasure room: a chest walled in by eight sealed blocks (12 HP, gold pick). A chest pays 300 + 150 per band + up to 400 coins, two gems, 1-2 crystal geodes and a 35% chance of a relic. 12% of deep floors also have a relic rock (gold pick), which always gives a relic you lack (300 coins and old coins once you have them all). There are eight relics: Stone Idol, Fossil Fish, Ancient Sword, Crystal Skull, Golden Scarab, Rune Tablet, Miner's Crown, and the Golem Heart, which only the Crystal Golem drops. The relic page opens from the elevator (RELICS); the full set pays 15,000 coins and a prismatic shard once.
- **Deep mine, part 4 (the Crystal Golem):** floor 80 is a lit hall with the Golem in the middle. Each phase it wears 3, 4, then 5 armour crystals (3 HP; a Crystal pick does 2 a tap, 1 energy a tap) while a ring at its feet charges its slam over 10, 8.5, then 7 seconds. When the ring fills it winds up for 1.2 s ("BACK OFF!": a tap now costs 6 energy) and slams. With armour still on, the slam costs 8 energy and regrows a crystal. With none left, it is stunned for 3.5 s with its heart bare ("STRIKE!"), and three hits end the phase. If the stun wears off first, the armour comes back. Beating all three phases gives 10,000 coins, 5 crystal bars, 3 diamonds, the Golem Heart relic and 1000 XP; there is no rematch. Running out of energy knocks the player out to B75, like critters.
- **Villager friendships (owner picks: gifts and orders, cutscenes, a unique gift, walking the village):** villagers the village has so far walk between its houses, shops and the Orders board, always by the village road, and go home at night (21:00-6:30). Tapping one opens their page: portrait, ten hearts, what they love, a chat once a day (+10) and a gift once a day. Gifts they love (their favourites) give +80, liked ones (the same kind of thing) +40, others +20, scraps (worth 4 coins or less) +5; silver and gold count 25% and 50% more. Delivered orders still add friendship. Hearts now go to ten (100, 250, 450, 700, 1000, 1350, 1750, 2200, 2700, 3250 points; old saves keep their hearts). At 2, 5 and 8 hearts a villager has a short story scene (a "!" over them; a button on their page). Each of the 33 scenes tells a little of their story and adds +30. At ten hearts each villager gives a unique keepsake decoration (+8 beauty, free to place, from a keepsakes page at the end of the Decorate shop).
- **Story Act 2 (owner picks: an agri-corp rival, weekly goal races, six chapters, a valley festival):** after the bank loan, Ms. Kessler of Vexmoor Agri-Corp wants the valley for mega-farms and challenges the farm to the Vexmoor Cup. Each game week (7 days) runs one contest: harvest, gold crops, orders, felled trees, animal products, orchard fruit, fish, jams and juices, or rocks mined (only ones the farm can enter). The score to beat is set by contest and chapter. Reaching it wins the week at once (800 + 400 per chapter coins); a week not won by its end goes to Vexmoor. Each chapter needs a Cup win plus a goal that ties in the new systems: 5 hearts with a villager, 4 orchard trees, 4 animals, 15 fish, then 50 beauty. Its scene (Kessler, with a portrait of her own) then plays on the farm. The sixth ends with the Valley Festival: Vexmoor leaves, a golden statue stands by the village road, and the farm gets 25,000 coins. The Cup page opens from the Goals panel (CUP).
- **Camera angle:** the owner looked at mockups of the farmyard in 3/4 top-down and straight overhead, and kept isometric for now. If it ever changes, 3/4 top-down is the cheaper switch: crops, trees, people and animals carry over; buildings, fences, ground tiles and the iso maths (drawing, tapping, depth, camera) don't.
- **Playtest round 6 fixes:** the fishing rod is now in the Workbench's TOOLS tab (it was missing, so fishing couldn't start); max-tier tools name their tier (Crystal); the orchard's sign is labelled ORCHARD so it isn't mistaken for Field E's; tapping an animal's card in the Pasture panel collects or pets it; hearts are drawn as little hearts.
- **Playtest round 7 fixes (deep mine, villagers and Act 2, mid-game sweep):** the Goals panel's CUP button opens the Vexmoor Cup (it only closed Goals, so the Cup page could not be reached; a touch test covers it); a dark floor with no torches says the glowing entry rides back up; the mine's tool chip names just the tier (CRYSTAL didn't fit with PICK); a gift is picked first (its name shows) and given with GIVE; villagers are easier to tap while they walk; grey text on the cream panels is darker; the menu is called MENU and DECORATE YOUR FARM is at its top (a tester only found it under the settings); free tiles for a decoration glow white (green vanished on the grass); the pasture has a sign by the Mine road (an egg once animals can be bought, the level before); orchard trees show "6 FRUIT EVERY 2 DAYS"; for a moment after a catch or a miss, taps on the river don't cast again (stray taps cost energy); order cards say XP and goal rewards show a coin.
- **Pet depth (owner, round 7):** the pet draws at its own depth again, so trees, crops, bushes and buildings in front of it hide it (it had been drawn over everything, so it showed through a tree's crown). When a tree or building covers most of it, a faint white outline over the world still shows where it is; its gift sparkle, hearts and fetch meter stay on top.

## Round 7: Rebuild The Village (owner picks, built in parts)
- **Name:** the game is now **Rebuild The Village**: app name, title, launch intro, settings footer and store art. The internal app ID stays, so installed copies update and keep their saves. The barn roof loses its DITHER FARM lettering.
- **Launch intro:** a fake loading screen (a bar with lines mixing farm puns and Grandpa's sayings), then a title reveal: the sun rises over ruined Willowmere and the title restores itself letter by letter. About 6 seconds, skippable, tap to start. The owner asked for designs and animation to go all out.
- **New story:** Grandpa **Elias** spent his life asking Goldcrest Bank for a loan to rebuild **Willowmere** and was refused every time by old Mr. Sterling. The opening has three flashbacks (young, middle-aged, old Elias at the bank), then a heavy deathbed scene in the farmhouse at dusk where he asks the player to rebuild the village (Mara and Tobias wait outside and greet the player after). The player then goes to the bank, and the young Mr. Sterling (the old one's son) refuses them too. His visits at each village tier stay; at the finale the loan is approved in Elias's name and the son breaks his family's habit. About 8 **letters** from Elias, tucked in his notebook, open at milestones (first harvest, village tiers, the mine, the first animal, the loan, the wedding, the kid). Story Act 2 ties in: when Elias was refused, desperate villagers sold land to Vexmoor, and Kessler wants the rest; winning the festival buys some back.
- **Player:** male or female, with two looks each (male: straw hat; short hair and green overalls. Female: blonde braids; long curly hair and a sun hat). Players with an old save pick once on first launch and keep their progress; Settings can replay the new opening.
- **Romance (opposite gender only, as the owner asked):** brides Ivy, Sela, Willa and Coco; grooms Rook and three new villagers: **Finn** the river fisher (arrives with fishing, level 6), **Jasper** the travelling fiddler (when the village is stone) and **Marco**, Kessler's surveyor who quits Vexmoor (during Act 2). Every candidate gets a new portrait with a blush version. At 8 hearts a bouquet starts dating, which unlocks hearts 9-10. Dating several is allowed: a bouquet or a loved gift to one costs the others about one heart, with a hurt line. Extra scenes: bouquet, a 10-heart date, the proposal, the wedding, and married scenes at 12 and 14 hearts. Nothing explicit: flirting, hand-holding, kisses, and a cut to black before the baby.
- **Proposal and wedding:** propose with a crafted ring (forged from a gold bar and a diamond), and only once the bigger farmhouse is built ("where would we even live?"). The wedding is three days later in the village square, with every villager there; Juniper leads it and mentions Elias.
- **Spouse:** lives at the farm, walks it by day and goes inside at night, and still keeps their shop for some hours. Each morning they do a random chore (water a field, feed the animals, tidy the forest, tend the orchard), may bring a morning gift (breakfast for energy, or something from their trade), and give a perk from their trade (e.g. Ivy: flowers and fruit grow faster; Rook: tools hit harder; Finn: fish bite sooner; Coco: dishes sell for more). Spouse hearts go to 14. Divorce is at the bank for 20,000 coins: they move back to the village at 4 hearts and can be wooed again.
- **Kid:** at 12 hearts the spouse asks; the baby arrives a few days later, a random boy or girl that the player names. One game season each as baby, toddler and kid; the kid follows the player and plays with the pet.
- **Order of work:** 1) rename, intro, new opening, gender; 2) new villagers and portraits; 3) dating, proposal, wedding; 4) spouse and divorce; 5) the kid. An APK after each.
- **Part 1, as built:** the launch intro (phone only; tests start on the farm) runs a 3.2 s fake loading bar (it stalls and lurches) under a wheat sprout that grows with it, with lines that mix farm puns and Grandpa's sayings; then the title: five sky keyframes crossfade from night to morning while the sun climbs from behind the hills and the land's light warms from night blue to full colour; the 14 letters drop in as grey, chipped, crooked boards, then are hammered back into nailed golden planks one by one (a white flash, a pop, wood chips, a sparkle, a clink); the ribbon with THE unfurls, a shimmer sweeps across, and the title waves gently with birds and petals. Any tap skips to the finished title. New music: a hopeful title theme and a slow minor elegy for the opening; new sounds: the stamp and the hammer. The opening is one scene (still "opening", so old saves and the Settings replay keep working) of about 70 lines: title cards, three flashbacks as sepia film (grain, scratches, flicker, dark corners) in the bank as it was forty years ago (an older poster, a calendar), each ending in the REFUSED stamp slamming down (a drop, a shake, dust, a thud); the picker; Grandpa's bedroom at dusk (the lamp's flickering glow, dust drifting in the window's beam, the room dimming line by line, his last lines typed slowly) until the lamp gutters out; a card; the porch at night (the lantern, fireflies, his empty rocking chair) with Mara and Tobias; and the bank today with the young Sterling. Faces talk while their line types and blink every few seconds; the speaker breathes. Every villager now has a bust (heart events use them too). The finale stamps APPROVED and names the loan for Elias. Six letters for now (first harvest, stone, the mine, first animal, brick, the loan): a sealed envelope chip under the trophy on the farm; opening one drops the envelope in, cracks the seal, opens the flap and slides the letter out, then writes it out in brown ink on old paper, signed, with his photo taped on. The Menu has GRANDPA'S LETTERS to read them again. Kessler's first scene and the festival now mention the land Vexmoor bought when Elias was refused, and the festival sells it back.
- **Part 2, as built (new villagers and faces):** Rook, Willa and Coco have their own young faces (they were recolours of Bram, Juniper and Sela): Rook with messy black hair, soot on his cheek and a leather apron; Willa with a blonde ponytail, freckles, a pencil behind her ear and plaid under denim; Coco with a big curly bun, gold earrings and an apron. Three newcomers: Finn the river fisher (level 6; sun-bleached hair under a teal bucket hat, a yellow raincoat; loves trout, salmon, pike, catfish and veggie stew), Jasper the travelling fiddler (level 8 and the stone village; auburn hair, a goatee, a green scarf; loves grape juice, honey, apple juice, strawberry tart and maple syrup) and Marco, Kessler's surveyor who quits to stay (from Act 2's third chapter, where Kessler calls him a traitor; neat side part, the Vexmoor jacket worn open; loves amethyst, emerald, quartz, pancakes and maple syrup). Each has everyday talk, three heart scenes (Finn and the broken weir, Jasper's song for the square, Marco's hand-drawn map with names instead of plot numbers) and a keepsake (Finn's tackle box, Jasper's spare fiddle, Marco's telescope), and walks the village. villagers.json marks each villager's gender and who can be courted (four women, four men). The eight candidates' faces have a blushing version (with its talking and blinking frames) for the romance scenes.
